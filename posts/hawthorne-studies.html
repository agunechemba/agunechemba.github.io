Long ago, in a big factory in a place called Hawthorne, the bosses wanted to know one thing: "How can we make our workers work harder?"

A group of clever scientists came to find out. "Let's test the lights!" they said.

So they made the lights brighter in one room. The workers made more parts!
"Aha!" said the scientists. "Brighter lights make people work harder!"

But wait — they had forgotten something. In another room, they had kept the lights the same. And guess what? Those workers also made more parts!

The scientists were confused. So they tried the opposite. They made the lights dimmer and dimmer in the first room. And the workers… still made more parts! Even when the light became so poor that the workers complained they could no longer see properly!

"What is happening?" the scientists asked. "It is not the lights at all!"

Then they discovered the secret. The workers were not working harder because of the lights. They were working harder because someone was paying attention to them. The bosses had never asked their opinions before. Now the scientists listened to them, asked them questions, and treated them like important people. The workers felt seen and valued. So they worked harder — happily!

The scientists learned a big lesson that day:

People are not machines. They are not just moved by money or bright lights. They care about being noticed, respected, and treated as part of a group. They have feelings, and those feelings change how they work.

This discovery started a whole new way of thinking called the Human Relations Approach — the idea that in any workplace, the human side matters most of all.
