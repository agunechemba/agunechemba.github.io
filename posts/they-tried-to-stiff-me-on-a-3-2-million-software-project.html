They tried to stiff me on a ₦3.2 million software project. Instead, they ended up paying me ₦2.7 million MORE and signing a monthly retainer.

Here is the story of why you never try to cut corners with the developer who built your infrastructure.

I built a comprehensive school management system for a private academy in Port Harcourt. It handled student records, report cards, fee payments, parent communication, and staff payroll.

The total contract was ₦3.2 million. They paid ₦1.5 million upfront, with the rest due after the first term.

The system was running perfectly. But the balance payment never came.

When I checked my server logs, I noticed a new admin account: "schooladmin2." It wasn't created by me or the proprietress. I traced the IP address to a residential area across town.

When I confronted the school, the proprietress dodged my calls. When we finally met (with her husband present), the husband—a self-proclaimed "oil and gas businessman"—smiled and said:
"We found someone who can maintain it for less. We'll pay what we think is fair based on the value received."

They had hired a fresh graduate for ₦50,000 to "maintain" the system while still owing me ₦1.7 million.

But that wasn't the worst part.

I dug into the code. The new developer wasn't maintaining the system—he was studying it. He was copying the database structure and frontend design to build his own version to sell to other schools.

Even worse, he introduced a massive vulnerability. He created a fake payment gateway that looked identical to the real one. Parents clicked "Pay Fees," the money left their accounts, and the system showed "Payment Successful."

But the funds weren't going to the school. They were going straight to the rogue developer's wallet. He had already stolen over ₦800,000 in fake payments.

I didn't call the proprietress. I called my lawyer.

The next morning, the school received a formal letter with screenshots of the unauthorized admin account, the fake gateway, and the transaction records showing the stolen funds. I demanded the outstanding balance plus damages.

The proprietress called me within an hour, her voice shaking. The husband called right after, shouting: "This is extortion!"

I calmly replied: "No. This is the cost of your mistake. The developer isn't the only one who committed fraud. You gave him access. You are liable."

Faced with police involvement, they went quiet.

I logged in, locked out the fake admin, deleted the fake payment gateway, restored the real one, forced 2FA on all accounts, and rebuilt the confirmation code. I secured the system I built.

Then, I sent the school a new invoice:

  • Balance: ₦1.7 million
  • Emergency security fix: ₦500,000
  • Data recovery and audit: ₦300,000
  • Legal consultation: ₦200,000
    Total: ₦2.7 million.

They paid in 22 hours.

Three days later, the rogue developer called me in tears. He begged for a second chance. "Can you teach me? I want to learn from you," he said.

"Sure," I replied. "My mentorship fee is ₦1 million per year. If you don't have it, start by returning the ₦800,000 you stole from the parents."

He disappeared shortly after.

As for the school? They now pay me a monthly fee for maintenance, security, and peace of mind. They learned the hard way.

The Business Takeaways:

  1. Never hand over full admin rights before final payment. Protect your architecture.
  2. Contracts are your best defense. When clients try to "restructure" agreements in bad faith, let the lawyers do the talking.
  3. You get what you pay for. The ₦50k "maintenance guy" cost them millions in fraud and a much higher invoice.
  4. Technical leverage is real. When you build the foundation, you hold the power to secure—or dismantle—it.
